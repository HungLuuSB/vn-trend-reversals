Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "Predictable but Mostly Mechanical: Survival Forecasts of Trend Reversals in Vietnamese Stocks" for consideration as a research article in the *Journal of Forecasting*.

The paper asks when a price trend ends, and how much of the answer can be forecast with information that was public before the market opened. We define trends as volatility-scaled directional-change swings of individual stocks in Vietnam's VN30 index and its listed banks. We forecast their reversal with landmark survival models, from the swing's own state, stock and local market features, overnight global markets, and, for banks, first-released fundamentals and news. Every design choice was fixed in a dated decision log on the years 2012–2016, and the test years 2017–2026 were used once, in a single walk-forward run.

The main finding is that reversal timing is predictable out of sample. The main model reaches a concordance index of 0.616 against 0.5 for Kaplan–Meier, with a lower integrated Brier score. However, most of this skill is mechanical: the distance to the reversal threshold alone accounts for 88% of the gain. Beyond the mechanics, stock and local market features improve the ranking of swings, but not their predicted probabilities. Overnight global information, bank fundamentals and news add nothing, swing age adds nothing once the distance is known, and the forecasts earn nothing in a long-only trading test after costs.

We believe the paper suits the journal for three reasons:

- It contributes a benchmark for turning-point forecasts. We pair a mechanical baseline built from the labelling rule with a random-walk null, and show that without them the apparent skill of reversal forecasts is easily overstated.
- It follows the forecasting literature's standards for honest evaluation. The paper uses pre-registered primary tests, chronological splits with purging and embargo, and shuffled-label controls. It reports every model and every failed hypothesis.
- It adds point-in-time evidence from a retail-dominated emerging market with daily price limits, where such forecasts are widely sold to investors.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. Both authors have approved the submitted version. The authors declare no conflicts of interest, and the research received no specific funding. The use of an AI assistant for code, reference checks and editing is disclosed in the manuscript. The data availability statement describes how the data and code can be obtained.

Thank you for considering our work.

Yours sincerely,
