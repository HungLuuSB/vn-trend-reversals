\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% Written after the results (skill order). Market facts are cited; every result is a generated
% macro from the single final run.

When does a price trend end? Traders ask it to time an exit, risk managers to size a position, and
regulators of retail-dominated markets ask it because many investors trade on signals that claim
to know. Vietnam is such a market. Individual investors account for 81.2\% of trading value on its
main exchange in 2024 \citep{SHS2025}, foreign investors for about a tenth \citep{HOSE2025}, and
9.3 million securities accounts were open by 2024, about 9\% of the population \citep{SSC2025}.
Daily price limits, settlement delays and the absence of short selling shape how prices move and
what an investor can do about it.

What is known about the question is mixed. Survival analysis of price reversals has been tried on
runs of daily index changes, and its forecasts were worse than a random walk out of sample
\citep{Yao2005}. Duration dependence of index bull and bear phases is well documented, but in
sample and with phase age as the only variable \citep{LundeTimmermann2004,Zakamulin2023}.
Machine-learning studies of directional-change events forecast trend lengths in currency markets
without treating open trends as censored \citep{AdegboyeKampouridis2021}, and survival models have
been used to filter trading signals for US stocks \citep{HuZhou2024}. Studies that report very high
accuracy for turning points use evaluation designs that can allow look-ahead
\citep{Abolmakarem2024}. What is missing is a test of whether the end of a stock's trend can be
forecast out of sample, with every input timed to when it became public, and with a benchmark that
tells apart real information from what the definition of a trend implies by itself.

This paper provides that test. We define a swing by a directional-change rule whose threshold is
twice the stock's trailing average true range, so swings of quiet and volatile stocks are measured
in the same units. Every open swing on every session is a censored spell, and landmark hazard
models forecast when it will be confirmed as reversed, from five blocks of features: the swing's
state, the stock's technicals, the local market, overnight global markets, and for banks, first
released non-performing-loan ratios and news attention. All features are timed to a decision at
the 09:00 open. Models are fitted on an expanding window, refitted each year, and tested once on
2017--2026 after all design work used 2012--2016 only. Besides Kaplan--Meier we compare every
model with two mechanical benchmarks: a flexible model of swing age and distance to the threshold,
and a random-walk null that rolls the stock's own recent returns through the labelling rule.

We ask five research questions. RQ1: is the timing of a swing's reversal predictable out of
sample, and beyond what the labelling rule implies? RQ2: does overnight global information add to
local information? RQ3: do trends age, that is, does a swing's age matter once its distance to the
threshold is known? RQ4: did the sensitivity to global shocks rise after the announcement of
Vietnam's market upgrade? RQ5: are the forecasts worth anything to a trader after costs? Each has a
pre-registered hypothesis, H1 to H5 (Table~\ref{tab:primary}), and banks get a further test of
their fundamentals and news.

The results separate what is predictable from what is mechanical. Reversal timing is predictable:
the main model reaches a year-stratified C-index of \CABCDx{} against 0.5 for Kaplan--Meier, with a
lower integrated Brier score, and its C-index stays between \YearMinABCDx{} and \YearMaxABCDx{} in
every test year. But the distance to the threshold alone delivers
\MechShareDIST\% of that gain. Beyond the mechanics, the stock's own recent behaviour and the local
market improve the ranking of swings in \YearsAboveABCxMFlex{} of 10 years, by \GainOverMFlex{} in C,
but they do not improve the predicted probabilities, so the pre-registered test of predictability
beyond the mechanics fails. Overnight global information, bank fundamentals and news add nothing;
swing age adds nothing once the distance is known; no change in global sensitivity is detected
after the market-upgrade announcement of October 2025; and an exit rule based on the forecasts
earns a Sharpe ratio of \BtOpenSharpeModel, below that of buy-and-hold (\BtOpenSharpeBh) and of
simply following the swings.

The paper makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A benchmark for turning-point forecasts.} Earlier survival and machine-learning studies
  compare their models with naive or unconditional baselines
  \citep{Yao2005,AdegboyeKampouridis2021,HuZhou2024}. We show that a mechanical baseline built from
  the labelling rule explains most of the apparent skill, and propose it, with a random-walk null,
  as the benchmark that such forecasts should beat.
\item \emph{Out-of-sample evidence where earlier work found none.} Unlike daily runs of index changes
  \citep{Yao2005}, volatility-scaled swings of individual stocks are predictable out of sample over
  ten years. We conjecture that the unit of analysis is the reason: the distance to the threshold
  is an informative state that a daily run lacks.
\item \emph{Age versus distance.} Age-only duration dependence of the kind estimated for index phases
  \citep{LundeTimmermann2004,Zakamulin2023} disappears once the distance to the threshold is known.
\item \emph{Global shocks in a frontier market, timed point in time.} With every global, fundamental
  and news input timed to when it was public, overnight shocks, bank fundamentals and news bring no
  detectable improvement to the timing of reversals in Vietnam, and no change is detected after its
  market-upgrade announcement, a test with low power.
\end{enumerate}
The whole design was fixed in a dated decision log before the test years were used, and every
pre-registered result comes from one run on a frozen data snapshot; the few later entries of the
log, and the post-hoc analyses, are listed in Section~\ref{sec:prereg}.
