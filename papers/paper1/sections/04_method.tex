\subsection{Swings: a volatility-scaled directional-change rule}\label{sec:swings}

A swing ends only when the price retraces a threshold that scales with the stock's own recent
volatility. The threshold for stock $i$ on session $t$ uses only the previous session:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
  \theta_{i,t} = k\,\frac{\mathrm{ATR}_{20}(i,t-1)}{P_{i,t-1}}, \qquad
  \mathrm{ATR}_{20}(i,t) = \frac{1}{20}\sum_{j=0}^{19} \mathrm{TR}_{i,t-j},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{TR}_{i,t}=\max(H_{i,t},P_{i,t-1})-\min(L_{i,t},P_{i,t-1})$ is the true range. The
average is a simple mean, not Wilder's smoothing. The main design fixes $k=2$; $k=1.5$ and $k=3$
are robustness checks. Prices are adjusted for dividends, splits and rights issues. Adjusted prices
enter only through returns and ratios, which later adjustments do not change (the adjustment
procedure is described in Section~\ref{sec:data}).

During an up-swing ($\delta_{i,t}=+1$) the running extreme $E_{i,t}$ is the highest close since the
swing's starting trough. The up-swing is confirmed as reversed on the first session $t$ on which

\begin{equation}\label{eq:confirm}
  P_{i,t} \le E_{i,t}\,(1-\theta_{i,t}).
\end{equation}
Down-swings mirror this with the running minimum and $P_{i,t}\ge E_{i,t}(1+\theta_{i,t})$. After a
confirmed reversal, the new swing's extreme is tracked from the old swing's extreme onward, so a
swing starts at the turning point, not at its confirmation. Before a stock's first confirmation,
both extremes are tracked and the first confirmation in either direction starts the first swing.

Two quantities describe where a swing stands after the close of $t$. The \emph{swing age}
$a_{i,t}$ counts sessions since the starting extreme. The \emph{distance to the threshold},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dist}
  d_{i,t} = \delta_{i,t}\,\frac{P_{i,t}/E_{i,t}-1}{\theta_{i,t}},
\end{equation}
is $0$ when the close sets a new extreme and reaches $-1$ when the reversal is confirmed. It
therefore lies in $(-1,0]$ on every session on which the swing is still open.

The rule is causal: confirmation and the threshold use only prices up to $t$. The future enters
only through the target, the time until the next confirmation. A swing is known only from its own
confirmation onward, so its first survival row has age $a_{i,t}>0$ (delayed entry). Because the
threshold is a multiple of each stock's own volatility, a swing of a volatile small bank and a
swing of a large, quiet stock are measured in the same units. This is what allows the swings of
different stocks to be pooled in one model.

\subsection{Survival rows and landmark hazards}\label{sec:survival}

Each row $(i,t)$ is one stock on one session inside a swing that has not yet been confirmed as
reversed. It describes the state after the close of $t$ and is used for a decision at the HOSE
open, 09:00 Vietnam time, on the next session. The outcome is $D_{i,t}$, the number of the stock's
sessions until the reversal is confirmed at a later close. A row is right-censored when the study
ends (the 2026-11-20 close), when the stock leaves the universe, or when the stock has no price for
five or more consecutive sessions (a trading halt). Shorter gaps are days without trades, and the
swing continues across them. A row enters the sample only if the stock has at least 250 sessions of
price history before $t$; this rule is applied point in time, so it does not depend on whether the
stock survives later.

The target is the hazard $s$ sessions after the decision, with the features held at their values on
day $t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard}
\begin{split}
  \lambda_s(x_{i,t}) &= \Pr\!\left(D_{i,t}=s \mid D_{i,t}\ge s,\ x_{i,t}\right),\\
  S(h\mid x_{i,t}) &= \prod_{s=1}^{h}\bigl(1-\lambda_s(x_{i,t})\bigr),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
for $s,h = 1,\dots,20$. This is a landmark formulation \citep{vanHouwelingen2007}: each session is
a new prediction for the same open swing, made with what is known on that session. Each row is
expanded into steps $s=1,\dots,\min(D_{i,t},20)$ with a binary target that equals one at the step
of the confirmation. Censored rows contribute their steps up to censoring. For fitting only,
durations above 21 sessions are censored at 21; no reported metric uses that cap.

The landmark form matters because the strongest features describe the current position of the
swing. A first implementation advanced only the age and held the distance $d_{i,t}$ fixed for
20 sessions. That chained hazard exaggerated both high and low risk and was worse calibrated than
Kaplan--Meier on the development folds. Estimating the hazard at each step after the decision, as
in equation~\eqref{eq:hazard}, removes the problem and matches how the prediction is used. The
correction was made on development data only and is logged.

\paragraph{Direction-specific effects} A linear hazard with a raw return gives that return the same
effect on up-swings and down-swings. The hypothesis on global shocks is directional: a US
fall should raise the hazard that an up-swing ends and lower the hazard that a down-swing ends.
Every feature $x$ in blocks B, C and D therefore enters the linear models both as $x$ and as
$\delta_{i,t}\,x$ (suffix ``x'' in specification names: \texttt{ABCx}, \texttt{ABCDx}). Block A is
already expressed relative to the swing. Tree and neural models receive the raw features and
can form these interactions themselves.

\paragraph{Bank interactions} The bank part of the global-shock hypothesis says that banks react more
to rates and the dollar. Specification \texttt{ABCDx\_bank} adds a bank indicator and its products
with the US 10-year yield change, the dollar index return and the USD/VND return, each also
multiplied by $\delta_{i,t}$.

\paragraph{Block E} For banks, the NPL ratio is groups 3--5 over the sum of the five loan groups of
the central bank's classification. It is read from the first-released consolidated statement of
each quarter, never from later restatements. A value is accepted only when the five groups add up to
the printed total in both columns of the table. Most values were read by optical character
recognition. Where the scan defeated it, the table was transcribed from the page image with an AI
assistant, under a stricter rule: the groups must equal the printed total exactly, in both columns.
Block E results are also reported without these transcribed values. A statement becomes usable at
09:00 on the fifth session after the later of its legal filing deadline and the PDF's creation
date. The rule is meant to be conservative (but see the disclosure in Section~\ref{sec:datalimits}). We checked it against the news: of the \BlockENewsMatched{} first-release statements whose result
could be matched to a news article, \BlockENewsLead{} were reported in the news before the statement's
usable time (median lead \BlockENewsLeadDays{} days). News counts are articles mentioning the bank published from 09:00 on $t$
to 09:00 on the next session. Section~\ref{sec:data} gives the sources.

\subsection{Mechanical baselines}\label{sec:mech}

Part of any skill at predicting reversals follows from the label itself. A swing close to its
threshold ($d_{i,t}$ near $-1$) needs only a small further move to be confirmed. A model that knows
$d_{i,t}$ and the stock's volatility can predict reversals without any behavioural content. We
therefore separate predictability \emph{beyond the mechanics} from predictability against
Kaplan--Meier, and use three mechanical baselines.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{M} is the logistic landmark hazard on age, $\log(1+a)$ and $d$, entered linearly.
\item \texttt{M\_flex} is a gradient-boosted landmark hazard on the same three features. It lets
  the mechanical relation take any shape, so a richer model cannot beat the mechanics merely by
  fixing the functional form of \texttt{M}.
\item \texttt{RW} is a model-free random-walk null with nothing fitted. For row $(i,t)$ we draw 500
  paths of 20 sessions. Each simulated session is a whole-day tuple (close-to-close log return,
  high/close, low/close) drawn with replacement from the stock's own last 250 sessions up to $t$.
  The directional-change rule is rolled forward from its exact state after the close of $t$: the
  direction, the running extreme, the close and the last 20 true ranges. The threshold is
  recomputed every simulated day from the simulated ATR20. $S(h)$ is the share of paths not yet
  confirmed as reversed by day $h$. A unit test checks that rolling the realised path reproduces the
  observed duration exactly.
\end{enumerate}

\texttt{RW} asks what reversal timing would look like if the stock's recent daily returns arrived in random
order. A model beats \texttt{RW} only if it knows something about the order of future returns that the
stock's own volatility does not.

\subsection{Models}\label{sec:models}

We fit six model families, from no features to a sequence model.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Kaplan--Meier} \citep{KaplanMeier1958}: one survival curve per training window, no
  features. Every model is compared with it.
\item \textbf{Logistic landmark hazard}, the main interpretable model: a logistic regression of
  the step target on step indicators and the features \citep{SingerWillett1993}, with an
  elastic-net penalty \citep{ZouHastie2005}. The main specification is \texttt{ABCDx}.
\item \textbf{Cox regression} \citep{Cox1972} with a ridge penalty, fitted to each row's time to
  reversal from the decision.
\item \textbf{Gradient-boosted landmark hazard}: histogram gradient boosting \citep{Friedman2001}
  on the same landmark expansion (300 iterations, L2 penalty 1).
\item \textbf{Random survival forest} \citep{Ishwaran2008}: 100 trees, square-root feature
  sampling.
\item \textbf{Neural hazard}: a gated recurrent unit \citep{Cho2014} reads the stock's own last 60
  sessions (log return, log high/low, log close/open, log volume relative to its 20-session mean),
  and its last state is joined with the static features. A hidden layer then outputs 20 step
  hazards, trained with the discrete-time likelihood \citep{KvammeBorgan2021}. The output bias
  starts at the training rows' empirical hazard. Training is deterministic on a CPU, and the final
  model averages the survival curves of three seeds.
\end{enumerate}

Every model imputes missing values with training medians and standardises features with training
moments. Hyperparameters are tuned by nested validation inside each training window. The last full
year before the test year is the inner validation year, with the same purge and embargo as the outer
split (Section~\ref{sec:walkforward}). The grid point with the lowest inner integrated Brier score is
refitted on the full training window. Grids are small and fixed in advance
(Appendix S1).

\subsection{Walk-forward protocol}\label{sec:walkforward}

The test years are 2017 to 2026, and each is one fold. The 2026 fold is a partial year that ends at the
2026-11-20 close. For test year $y$:

\begin{itemize}
\item the training window holds every row from 2012-01-03 to the end of year $y-1$ (an expanding
  window, refitted each January);
\item training rows whose swing ends on or after 1 January of $y$ are dropped (the purge), so no
  training outcome overlaps the test period;
\item the 20 sessions before 1 January of $y$ are dropped as well (the embargo);
\item the model is tuned and fitted on that window and then predicts every row of year $y$.
\end{itemize}

All design work, including every specification choice in this section, used only the
development period 2012--2016, which lies inside the first training window. The test years were
touched once, by a single run of the complete protocol on a frozen data snapshot.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

Ranking is measured by Uno's concordance index \citep{Uno2011}, truncated at $\tau=20$ sessions,
with the risk score $1-S(20\mid x)$. Accuracy of the whole curve is measured by the integrated Brier
score (IBS) over days 1 to 20, with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights \citep{Graf1999}. Both
take their censoring distribution $\hat G$ from the training outcomes of the same fold.

\paragraph{Year-stratified metrics (primary)} A C-index pooled over ten test years mixes ten
separately fitted models. Most of its comparable pairs then cross years, and those pairs
reward differences in reversal rates between years rather than ranking within them. The primary C
therefore adds up the weighted concordant and comparable pairs within each year only:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stratc}
  C^{\mathrm{strat}}_\tau = \frac{\sum_y \sum_{i,j\in y} \Delta_i\,\hat G_y(T_i)^{-2}\,
    \mathbf{1}\{T_i<T_j,\ T_i<\tau\}\,\mathbf{1}\{r_i>r_j\}}
  {\sum_y \sum_{i,j\in y} \Delta_i\,\hat G_y(T_i)^{-2}\,\mathbf{1}\{T_i<T_j,\ T_i<\tau\}},
\end{equation}
where $T_i$ is the observed time, $\Delta_i$ the event indicator, $r_i$ the risk score and $\hat
G_y$ the censoring survival function from year $y$'s training outcomes. Ties in $r$ count one half.
With a single year this equals Uno's C exactly. The primary IBS is the row-weighted mean of the
yearly IBS, each with its own year's $\hat G_y$. The pooled C and IBS, comparable with the
development results, and a C computed within dates (cross-sectional ranking only) are reported as
secondary.

\paragraph{Secondary metrics} Time-dependent AUC at 5, 10 and 20 sessions; calibration at the
same horizons by deciles of predicted risk, with Kaplan--Meier observed rates in each decile.

\subsection{Inference and decision rules}\label{sec:inference}

\paragraph{Paired block bootstrap} Rows of the same swing and the same date are dependent. We
resample test dates in moving blocks of 20 sessions \citep{Kunsch1989}, with every model scored on
the same resamples, and recompute each metric on each resample. Differences between models are
therefore paired. Blocks are drawn from the pooled test dates and may span the turn of a year;
each resampled row keeps its own year's censoring weights. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals. Blocks of 40 and 60 sessions are a
sensitivity check, since many swings last longer than 20 sessions.

\paragraph{Decision rule} A model counts as an improvement over another only if the interval of the
C difference lies above zero \emph{and} the interval of the IBS difference lies below zero. The
rule asks for better ranking and a better curve at the same time. As a conjunction of one-sided
tests it is an intersection--union test, valid at the nominal level, and conservative, without adjustment
\citep{Berger1982}.

\paragraph{One primary test per question} Table~\ref{tab:primary} lists the primary tests. Each
uses 1,000 paired resamples. We report its Monte Carlo error as the spread of the interval endpoints
between the two halves of the resamples. The bootstrap $p$-value of a primary is the larger of
two one-sided shares: the resamples in which C does not rise, and those in which the IBS does not
fall. Holm-adjusted bootstrap $p$-values across the
confirmatory primaries are reported as a sensitivity \citep{Holm1979}. Every
other comparison is secondary or descriptive and is labelled so.

\paragraph{H3 with a flexible distance} The age effect is tested given the distance, and the
distance enters both arms flexibly. \texttt{DIST\_s} is a cubic B-spline of $d_{i,t}$ with knots
fixed in advance at $-1$, $-0.75$, $-0.5$, $-0.25$ and $0$. Since $d$ lies in $(-1,0]$ by
construction, no knot depends on the data. \texttt{M\_s} adds age and $\log(1+a)$. Trends ``age''
only if \texttt{M\_s} beats \texttt{DIST\_s}.

\paragraph{Forecast-loss tests} As descriptive companions we report Diebold--Mariano tests
\citep{DieboldMariano1995} on the daily mean Brier loss of the 5-session reversal probability, with
Newey--West variances \citep{NeweyWest1987}. For nested pairs within one model family, we use the
adjustment of \citet{ClarkWest2007}.

\paragraph{Attribution within block D} Each of the 14 global features is dropped in turn from
\texttt{ABCDx}, refitted with that year's \texttt{ABCDx} hyperparameters, and compared on IBS with
2,000 resamples and \citet{Holm1979} adjustment across the 14. A separate contrast asks whether gold adds
anything once geopolitical risk is in the model, and the reverse.

\paragraph{Controls and stop rules} For \texttt{ABCD}, \texttt{ABCDx}, \texttt{M} and the neural model, the
outcome pairs (duration, event) are permuted across training rows and the model is refitted. A
model trained on permuted outcomes should rank test rows at chance. The run was set to stop before
any other output was read if the mean over years of the shuffled C exceeded 0.53, or if any model's
C exceeded 0.70. Either would point to leakage, and a stop would trigger a documented audit.

\subsection{In-sample tests of direction and of the market upgrade}\label{sec:insample}

Two questions are about the sign or the change of an effect, not about forecasting. We answer them
with full-sample estimates and say so.

\paragraph{Signs of the global effects (H2, directional part)} Specification
\texttt{ABCDx\_bank} is fitted as a landmark logit on all rows from 2012 to 2026-11-20, with a light ridge (inverse
penalty 100) only so that it converges. Rows of one swing overlap, so standard errors are clustered
by date and, separately, two-way by date and stock \citep{CameronGelbachMiller2011}. The larger
$p$-value decides. The pre-registered signs are negative for the S\&P 500 and Nasdaq terms
multiplied by direction, and positive for the VIX term. Each is tested one-sided, with Holm
adjustment across the three. The bank part is a joint Wald test of the six bank interaction terms.
We also count the yearly \texttt{ABCDx} fits whose coefficient has the predicted sign.

\paragraph{Market upgrade (H4, exploratory)} FTSE Russell announced Vietnam's upgrade to Secondary
Emerging status on 7 October 2025, effective 21 September 2026. Only the 2026 refit has any
post-announcement training data, so an out-of-sample test would have almost no power. We fit the
block-D linear index $\eta_D$ of \texttt{ABCDx} on the rows before the announcement. We then
estimate \texttt{ABCDx} plus a post-announcement indicator and its product with $\eta_D$ on the
four years before the announcement and the months after it. H4 predicts a positive coefficient
$\lambda$ on that product, tested one-sided with the conservative clustered errors above. Placebo
announcements on 7 October of each year from 2016 to 2023 use matched four-year pre-windows and
post-windows of up to 13 months that end before the real announcement. H4 is supported only if the
one-sided $p<0.05$ and the real statistic exceeds every placebo. A joint Wald test of ten
post-announcement interactions is secondary.

\subsection{Trading test}\label{sec:trading}

The trading test asks whether the predicted hazard is worth anything after Vietnamese trading
frictions. Short selling of individual stocks was not available during the sample
(Section~\ref{sec:market}), so every strategy is
long-only.

\paragraph{Strategies} Each strategy holds at most one position per stock.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Model exit} (H5): buy at the open after an up-swing is confirmed. Sell at the open after
  the first day on which \texttt{ABCDx}'s predicted 5-session reversal probability exceeds $\tau$,
  or after the down-reversal is confirmed, whichever comes first.
\item \emph{Mechanical exit}: the same rule with the predictions of \texttt{M}.
\item \emph{Directional-change exit}: the same entry; sell at the open after the down-reversal is
  confirmed.
\item \emph{Buy-and-hold}: hold every stock while it is in the universe.
\end{enumerate}
The threshold $\tau\in\{0.25,0.30,\dots,0.60\}$ is chosen each year on the inner validation year, by
the net Sharpe ratio at the main cost; ties go to the larger $\tau$.

\paragraph{Frictions} Shares bought cannot be sold before settlement: T+3 for trades up to
2015-12-31 and T+2 from 2016-01-04. A buy does not fill if the raw open is at or above the
ceiling, and a sell does not fill if it is at or below the floor; unfilled orders are retried at the
next open. The limits are those in force on each date, computed from the exchange reference price.
Where the limit cannot be computed, the order is assumed to fill and the day is flagged; a
sensitivity case forbids trading on such days. The main round-trip cost is 0.40\% (0.15\% brokerage
per side plus the 0.10\% tax on sales); 0.20\% and 0.80\% are sensitivities. A second execution
variant trades at the close of the session after the signal instead of the open.

\paragraph{Portfolio and metrics} The portfolio is equal-weighted across the day's universe stocks,
each slot earning the stock's open-to-open return when held and zero otherwise. We report the
annualised return and Sharpe ratio (risk-free rate zero), maximum drawdown, turnover and the
number of trades. H5 is supported only if the model exit beats all three benchmarks, each with a
paired block-bootstrap interval of the Sharpe difference above zero at 0.40\% costs. We also report
the probabilistic Sharpe ratio \citep{BaileyLopezdePrado2012} and the deflated Sharpe ratio
\citep{BaileyLopezdePrado2014}, with $N=16$ trials as pre-registered and with every trading
configuration actually computed in the run.

\subsection{Pre-registration and number of configurations}\label{sec:prereg}

Every design decision is recorded in a dated log before it was used, with the state of the
evidence at that moment; the record is available with the code (see the data availability
statement). The log is kept by the authors and is not time-stamped by a third party. The development
period produced \gen{DevRunCount} logged runs, summarised in Appendix S1; the full run log is available with the code. One
change to the evaluation came from development results: the mechanical baseline was added after
the distance to the threshold alone was found to carry most of the ranking skill. A second change,
direction-specific effects, followed from the stated direction of H2. After a design review and
before any test-year output, the mechanical nulls \texttt{M\_flex} and \texttt{RW}, the year-stratified primary
metrics and the stricter H5 rule were added. None of these changes could use test-year
information, because the test years had not been evaluated.

Four entries were made after the final per-year fits. The first fixed a crash in the evaluation code:
a secondary metric, the C-index within dates, failed on a date with no reversal before $\tau$. The
crash stopped the run before any metric was written, when only the shuffled-label controls of the
stop rules had been read; the fix changed no primary metric. Two recorded checks of the
legal sources of the market rules and the disclosure of Section~\ref{sec:datalimits}; they changed
no input of the frozen snapshot. The last added the analyses marked \emph{post hoc} in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, which re-score the cached test-year predictions without refitting any
model and change no pre-registered decision.
