\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked when price swings of Vietnamese stocks end, and how much of the answer can be forecast
with what is public before the market opens. Using volatility-scaled directional-change swings,
landmark survival models and a single pre-registered walk-forward test over 2017--2026, we find
that reversal timing is predictable out of sample against Kaplan--Meier, for every threshold, with
a higher C than Kaplan--Meier in every test year, although in 2022 the gain does not pass the
decision rule. Most of that predictability is mechanical: the distance to the reversal threshold
explains most of it, and a flexible function of age and distance is as well calibrated as the
richer linear models. Stock-level and local market features improve the ranking of swings; in the
pre-registered linear model they do not improve the probabilities, while, post hoc, non-linear models
of the same features improve both by small margins. Overnight global information, bank fundamentals
and news attention bring no detectable improvement, nor does swing age once the distance is known; no
change in global sensitivity is detected after the
market-upgrade announcement, and the forecasts earn nothing in a long-only trading test after
costs.

For researchers, the lesson is to compare any forecast of turning points with the forecast that the
labelling rule implies by itself. A mechanical baseline and a random-walk null that rolls the
stock's own returns through the rule are simple to build and change the reading of the results. For
practitioners and for regulators of retail-dominated markets, the results caution against signals
that promise to time reversals: what can be timed is mostly what the definition of a reversal
already tells. Future work could extend the test to markets that allow short selling, to intraday
data, and to direct measures of US monetary surprises and foreign order flows.
