\subsection*{S1.1 Hyperparameters}

test year), by the lowest inner integrated Brier score, and refitted on the full training window.
Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists the grids, fixed before the development work, and how often each
setting was chosen across the 10 test years.

\subsection*{S1.4 Extraction of bank statements}
Non-performing-loan ratios come from \BlockEStatements{} first-release quarterly statements, of
which \BlockECoverage{} yield a ratio: \BlockEOCR{} read by optical character recognition and
\BlockEVisual{} transcribed from page images under the stricter sum check described in the main text
(Section 4.3).

\subsection*{S1.6 Random versus chronological splits}
Abolmakarem et al.\ (2024) report next-day direction accuracy of 95.8--97.0\% and a test $R^2$ of
about 0.9999 for daily returns. We rebuilt their 28 inputs (15 log-price ratios over the last four
days and 13 technical indicators; level indicators divided by the close so that stocks can be
pooled) and three of their targets on our development years only: next-day direction (Trend), peak
detection, and the daily return. A one-hidden-layer perceptron with 40 units was fitted on
\AbolRows{} stock-days of \AbolStocks{} stocks under two splits: random (70/15/15 over rows, the
default of the software their training setup suggests) and chronological (the first 70\% of dates
for training). As a diagnostic, not part of the pre-registered comparison, we also aligned each
target with the inputs of the same day instead of the previous day; their first input,
$\ln(C_t/C_{t-1})$, then contains the answer.

Table~\ref{tab:abol} shows the result. With correct alignment, direction accuracy equals the
majority-class rate under both splits (\AbolRandomNextTrend{} against \AbolRandomNextTrendBase{},
and \AbolChronologicalNextTrend{} against \AbolChronologicalNextTrendBase), and the $R^2$ of the
daily return is about zero (\AbolRandomNextRet{} and \AbolChronologicalNextRet). Aligning the target
with the same day reproduces accuracy of the published size under either split
(\AbolRandomSameTrend{} and \AbolChronologicalSameTrend; $R^2$ \AbolRandomSameRet). In this design
the type of split matters little; the alignment of target and inputs decides the result. Peak
detection scores above its majority class even with correct alignment, because half of its
definition, whether the previous day closed up, is already among the inputs: the same mechanical
effect that the main analysis controls for with the mechanical baseline.
